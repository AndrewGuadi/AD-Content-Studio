# Script-level review of prior Authentic Dynamics reels
Date: 2026-10-29
Basis: user-supplied file `All Posts — Outline & Video Transcripts.md` (compiled Oct 20; Whisper large-v3 transcripts). This review concerns **words and narrative structure only**, not frames, audio quality, facial delivery, on-screen visuals or Instagram analytics. The five newer series reels have no verbatim transcripts in this file and are **not evaluated**.

## Observed strengths
- Sep 20, *Digital, Explained — introduction* (52s): opens with familiar owner frustrations—website, SEO, business-profile verification—and promises to explain the 'why'. It successfully positions the founder as a local educator. Risk: several example topics in one hook may dilute the central promise.
- Sep 21, *Do You Actually Own Your Website?* (42s): strong direct question followed by a precise test—whether a business can leave its provider and retain the work. Tight, differentiated positioning; closing 'We build. You own.' is earned rather than bolted on. Factual caveat: ownership and portability can have contractual/platform constraints; avoid universally asserting that providers own assets.
- Sep 22, *Website, Domain & Hosting* (63s): effective address / location / user-facing object analogy. Valuable practical distinction with a purchasing consequence. A brief diagram could carry information better than additional narration.
- Sep 23, *Your Domain Is a Business Asset* (49s): connects domain name to broader business continuity and control. The blanket 'Who is it registered to when you look it up?' is less reliable as a simple public-lookup method due to privacy/proxy records; prefer registrar-account and registrant-control checks.
- Sep 24, *A Pretty Website Can Still Be a Bad Website* (57s): strongest quick checklist in the dataset; customer-centered questions on service area, phone usability and next action; suited to carousel companion.
- Sep 25, *Real estate live search* (45s): grounded first-person search walkthrough is a valuable difference from generic SEO advice. Search order and results vary with location, device, personalization and time. A single search shouldn't justify a general conclusion that SEO investment is ineffective; explain it as an illustrative observation and recommend measuring actual customer acquisition.
- Sep 28, *People Aren't Searching in One Place Anymore* (45s): useful multi-platform customer-journey idea. The spoken 75% BrightLocal 2026 claim needs source verification before repurposing. Good potential to connect business profiles, reviews, website and social.
- Sep 28, *Citadels/Jackbox-inspired demo* (149s): distinct evidence of creative engineering; visible concept likely stronger than talking-only content, but speech transcript has stops, repetition and in-progress mistakes. A short cut around 'problem → phone-as-hand → live result' could make the technical payoff clearer to strangers.
- Sep 30, *Search Ads vs Local Services Ads* (60s): informative differentiation and appropriate caution that eligibility/screening depend on category and location. Strong fit for paid-ad literacy; recheck program requirements at publication date.
- Oct 20, *Still answering the same questions?* (26s): clear pain points and simple value proposition, but the transcript itself doesn't demonstrate a website answering any question. Strong candidate for a before/after sample.

## Cross-cutting editorial recommendations
1. State a viewer problem in first 3 seconds; answer one question instead of many.
2. Use concrete before/after screen demos to support explanations; reserve founder face-to-camera time for context and trust.
3. Finish with one useful viewer action rather than multiple repeated greetings and CTAs.
4. Verify statistics, prices and platform-specific terms; distinguish a one-off personal search observation from general claims.
5. Trim unclear repetitions, not authentic personality. Keep local warmth and everyday language.
6. Avoid repeating near-identical recordings as separate feed items accidentally; differentiate reel vs story repurposing in tracking.

## Prioritization
**Best scripts to adapt or build on:** website ownership, pretty-vs-effective site, and real estate search (with more careful framing).
**Best demos to shorten/clarify:** game controller concept.
**Best topics needing extra sourcing:** cross-channel search statistics and platform-ad eligibility.
**Cannot assess:** visual consistency, actual hook retention, subtitle placement, audio, editing or post performance.
